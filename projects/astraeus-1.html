Astraeus 1

A model-rocket flight system currently in design, with custom avionics, redundant sensing, live telemetry and parachute recovery.

Role
Solo project
Timeline
Dec 2025 - ongoing
Stack
RPi Pico 2 · LoRa · GPS · KiCAD
Status
Design phase · nothing built yet
Limits
20 g motor · 120 m ceiling

In Germany this rocket gets 20 g of propellant and 120 m of altitude.

Outside a club that is the limit: 20 g per flight without an ascent permit, 120 m ceiling. Not much room to make the flight itself impressive.

So the interesting part is what the rocket does inside that box. Astraeus 1 logs altitude, acceleration and attitude, sends them to a ground station while it flies, and releases its own parachute from sensor data instead of a delay charge burning down.

The motor decided most of the design.

A Klima C6-P: 10 Ns impulse, 6 N average thrust, 1.6 s burn. Klima gives a maximum lift-off mass of 160 g and my target is 175 g, but that figure assumes a launch rod of about a metre. On a 3 m rail the heavier rocket still leaves at roughly 17.9 m/s instead of 12.8 m/s, fast enough for the fins to stabilise it. That is why I am building a rail instead of using a rod.

If it comes out heavy, the order is already decided: smaller servo, smaller battery, lighter parachute, about 16 g between them.

Two of everything the parachute depends on.

Two BMP388 barometers and two LSM6DSOX IMUs, compared against each other, with a disagreeing sensor flagged instead of averaged in.

Apogee is read from barometric vertical speed across both barometers, behind a minimum time and altitude gate and several confirming samples. A timeout taken from the simulated flight profile can also trigger it, but only once launch has been confirmed. Too early and the parachute tears off at speed, too late and it opens near the ground.

The flight computer never touches the igniter.

Ignition stays separate hardware: certified igniters and a commercial launch controller at an approved site. Nothing on the board can reach that circuit.

The firmware runs nine states, from BOOT/SELFTEST to FAULT. In SAFE the deployment pin is high-impedance and a mechanical pin has to be pulled by hand before the servo can move at all. Once the rocket is flying the ground station can read telemetry but cannot command anything.

Nothing is glued.

No epoxy in the avionics bay. Modules plug into JST connectors on a printed tray with M2 screws, so sensors can be unplugged, tested on the bench and reused in the next rocket. How they are mounted is also a measurement decision: IMUs rigid and aligned, GPS on foam, barometer port shielded from direct airflow but not sealed.

The board is four layers in KiCAD with one continuous ground plane, since a switching converter, an 868 MHz transmitter and two IMUs share it. Sensors and radio run on separate 3.3 V rails so a transmit burst cannot drag the IMUs down with it.

On the pad the only interface is a piezo.

Three short beeps: sensors and battery good. Four short: SD card missing or unwritable. Two short, repeating: a sensor failed to start. Silence: the Pico is not running. There is no continuous low-battery tone, because a buzzer left on drains the battery it is warning about.

Where it stands.

Astraeus 1 is currently schematics, a parts list and a current budget. Next is the PCB layout, the airframe CAD, an OpenRocket run for stability and rail-exit speed, then parts and bench testing - including the failures on purpose: sensors unplugged, barometers forced to disagree, resets in every flight state.

A second version with a camera, a small payload and thrust vector control is sketched out, and may become a Jugend forscht entry in 2028/2029. The first flight is called Above and Beyond.
