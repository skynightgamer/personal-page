I build things to understand how they work—and to see where an idea can go.

I’m a student at Maria-Theresia-Gymnasium in Munich, graduating in 2028. Alongside school, I have taken part in TUM’s early-study programme in computer science (Frühstudium Informatik) since October 2025. My coursework has included Einführung in die Informatik (EIDI), Grundlagenpraktikum Programmierung (PGDP), Einführung in die Softwaretechnik (EIST) and Funktionale Programmierung und Verifikation (FPV).

I’m most drawn to work that needs genuine creativity, real effort and a good team. My projects move between software, electronics and media; I enjoy the whole process, from getting the hardware right and writing the firmware to reshaping an interface or finding the clearest edit in imperfect footage. Focusing on only one part has never held my interest for long.

I also build small projects simply to find out whether an idea will work. One of them was a QR-based room planner for my school: scanning a code outside a room opened its current timetable, so timetable changes would not require replacing paper displays. It was useful to prototype, but it also showed me the limits of a technically working solution: it needed upkeep and depended on students being able to use their phones. That kind of honest feedback is as valuable to me as a project going exactly to plan.

That approach shapes projects such as Astraeus 1, a model-rocket flight system currently in design; TaskMinder, a school platform used by more than 80 people daily; a real-time 2D physics engine written in C++; and an Ender 3 V2 I rebuilt from the base plate up. Space has interested me for as long as I can remember, but watching channels like BPS.space made it feel like something I could actually build myself.

I lead weekly CAD, 3D-printing and robotics courses for other students. The recognition that matters most to me is hearing directly from someone about what they liked, used or learned from something I helped create. Mathematics, computer-science competitions and practical engineering each give me a different way to work through a difficult problem and turn it into something real.

Away from engineering, I have played piano for 13 years and tennis for 8. I also shoot and edit films and photographs. That started with a rushed school horror film made with six to eight classmates. After an unsuccessful first attempt, we abandoned the idea and restarted with about 24 hours left. I worked as camera operator with an iPhone 13 and the Blackmagic camera app, then handled much of the editing and colour correction in DaVinci Resolve. The inconsistent footage forced me to learn how much can—and cannot—be repaired in post. The final film is visibly a first project, but the experience left me interested in cinematography and still shapes the photography I do now.
